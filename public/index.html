About Parkinson’s Disease

1. What is Parkinson’s Disease?

Parkinson’s disease is a chronic neurodegenerative disorder of the central nervous system. It primarily affects dopamine-producing neurons located in a specialized region of the midbrain called the substantia nigra. The depletion of dopamine causes impaired motor coordination and tremors.

2. Common Symptoms

• Tremor: Involuntary resting tremors in limbs or hands.
• Rigidity: Stiffness and resistance to movement in arms, legs, or trunk.
• Bradykinesia: Characteristic slowness in initiating voluntary physical movement.
• Postural Instability: Impaired balance and increased fall risk.
• Dysphonia & Hypophonia: Monotone pitch, quiet volume, vocal tremors, and hoarseness.

3. Why Early Detection Matters

Because standard physical symptoms emerge only after 60%–80% of dopamine neurons are lost, detecting subtle micro-symptoms earlier enables timely medical management, targeted lifestyle and physical therapies, and improved long-term quality of life.

4. Role of Voice Measurements

Over 90% of individuals with Parkinson’s develop voice changes (dysphonia). The delicate laryngeal muscles governing vocal cord tension show micro-tremors and instability long before limb tremors appear. Acoustic measures like Jitter, Shimmer, Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio (HNR), and Pitch Period Entropy (PPE) quantitatively capture these vocal fold irregularities.

5. Limitations of AI-Based Prediction

While machine learning models achieve high accuracy on standard benchmarks, voice tests are susceptible to background noise, colds, smoking, and throat strain. They serve as an educational screening tool and must never replace clinical neurological diagnosis.
